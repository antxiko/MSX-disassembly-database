The finding is not ours

It was uncovered by Manuel Pazos (@ManuelPazosMSX) in September 2021. Thanks to him we know to look there.

Konami hid the catalogue number and the katakana title inside many of its MSX cartridges. Read forwards, the block goes:

[the title, N bytes, in reverse order] · [N] · [the two RC digits in BCD] · [0xAA]

The sweep runs over all forty-nine binaries, including the ones that are not Konami's. That is the control: if the tracer found the mark on a game that is not Konami's, it would be swallowing coincidences. It finds none.

The syllables are the house codes, one per byte, with the " and o diacritics kept apart. That is how the tracer gives it and how it is stored; reading it back as kana is the reader's doing, not the binary's.

All seventeen give the number their card publishes

Two independent paths to the same fact: what Konami left inside the cartridge and what the series publishes outside. They agree on all seventeen, and a test watches it.

And not every cartridge has one

These are Konami's and do not carry it: Time Pilot, Frogger, Super Cobra, Athletic Land, Monkey Academy, Konami’s Billiards, Konami’s Mahjong Dojo, Hyper Olympic 1, Hyper Olympic 2, Sky Jaguar, Konami's Golf, Yie Ar Kung-Fu, Konami's Tennis, Hyper Sports 1, Hyper Sports 2, Cabbage Patch Kids, F-1 Spirit — The Way to Formula 1. That absence is a fact about the series, not a measurement failure.
