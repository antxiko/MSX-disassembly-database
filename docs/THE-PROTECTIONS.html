The copy protections

A cartridge writing inside its own space is doing nothing at all: ROM takes no writes, so that instruction looks like dead code. It is not. A pirated cartridge is a copy loaded into RAM, and there the write does land and leaves the game broken somewhere it never returns from.

Whose finding this is

The pattern was identified by Manuel Pazos (@ManuelPazosMSX) in his disassembly of King's Valley (RC-727), where he named the two routines ReadKeys_AC and VRAM_writeAC. What is here is that same pattern, looked for across the 40 cartridges in the series.

13 of the 40 write inside their own space, and all 13 are Konami. The family always repeats the same two, in the same two start-up routines: one puts a pop hl and a ret over a djnz in the presentation chain, the other leaves a zero in the operand of a jp, which in memory turns it into jp 00000h —a dead reset—. That the target is always a djnz or the operand of a jump, and never a gap in the data, is what rules out stray writes.

The ones aimed at the BIOS ROM

Page 0 of an MSX is ROM too, so a write there does not land either. But going to the BIOS does not make a write a protection: 2 turn up, and each is either explained or marked as unexplained.

Antarctic Adventure. It is the start-up guard: it copies jp 0000h over the BIOS entry point. A fourth build of the game proves it is a protection: identical save for two bytes, exactly the ones that send that same copy to DESPACHA, inside the cartridge itself.

Hyper Rally. Unexplained. It lands in eighteen bytes that, read raw, look like two nine-byte rows of data (2F 30 34 32 2F 2F 2B 2C 0F), reached through a pushed return address. Whether it is real code or data traced as code needs the emulator; until then it is not counted as a protection.

The ones checked that carry none

Of Konami's, 20 cartridges write neither to their own space nor to the BIOS: Athletic Land, Cabbage Patch Kids, F-1 Spirit — The Way to Formula 1, Frogger, Hyper Olympic 1, Hyper Olympic 2, Hyper Sports 1, Hyper Sports 2, Konami's Baseball, Konami's Golf, Konami’s Billiards, Konami’s Game Master, Konami’s Mahjong Dojo, Monkey Academy, Nemesis / Gradius, Road Fighter, Sky Jaguar, Super Cobra, Time Pilot, Yie Ar Kung-Fu.

And none of the 6 cartridges that are not Konami's carries any of this (3D Golf Simulation, Bomber Man, Hole in One, Hole in One Professional, Casio World Open, Pitfall!). That is the control: if the sweep swallowed coincidences, they would show up there too.

The two MegaROMs, Nemesis / Gradius (KONAMI), F-1 Spirit — The Way to Formula 1 (SCC), are checked differently: their listing goes bank by bank, so their own space is the whole window, 0x4000–0xBFFF, minus the registers of their mapper, worked out from their own writes. Everything they write there is a bank switch.

What this does NOT say

What actually happens when a copy runs in RAM is not measured: that would mean loading a copy and playing it, and no binary is distributed here. What is claimed is what the listing says: where the write is, where it points and what instruction sits at the target.

Nor are these all of them: the sweep sees writes to a fixed address, and those going through HL or DE loaded just before. One computing its target on the fly would slip past.

The binaries are not distributed. This repository holds measurements and analysis, not the games. Each game belongs to its authors.
