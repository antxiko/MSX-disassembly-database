What they share looks for identical runs of bytes, so a shared routine only shows up there if it was also assembled at the same address. Here the listings are compared instruction by instruction: a block is the instructions from a label to its ret or its unconditional jump, at least six of them and three that are not call, jp or ret, with the 16-bit operands and the names covered, because they change from one cartridge to the next. Two cartridges share a block if it is in both listings. The labels inside a shared block give tails that match as well: of those ending at the same instruction only the longest counts.

These are the 65 Konami disassemblies in the series, measured on 25 October 2026 (Space Manbow, on the 29th) with tools/codigo_comun.py. Of the 2,080 possible pairs, 1,398 share at least one block and 464 share ten or more. A lone six-instruction block can come out the same without being copied: what says two cartridges carry the same code is sharing a lot of them.
